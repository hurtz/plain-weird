Laserdisc Teardown

How the movie games of 1983 to 1991 actually worked, what got cut from Dragon's Lair before release, every move to finish it, and how you would build one now.

The short version. Dragon's Lair is a cartoon stored on a giant shiny disc. A small computer in the cabinet watches your joystick. Each scene has a tiny window where one move is right.

Hit it and the computer lets the disc keep playing. Miss it and the computer tells the player to jump to a death clip, then jump back. That jumping is called seeking, and it is what wore the players out.

Max score, one life, arcade Hard
Free points for entering any room

A 12 inch CAV disc spins once per video frame, 1,800 times a minute, and every one of its 54,000 frames per side has its own address. That is the whole trick. Dragon's Lair used one side; the back was metal to keep the disc flat.

Chapter 1

How the trick works

There are no graphics chips drawing Dirk. Everything you see is video. The game program is a table of frame numbers and timing windows, and its only job is to tell the disc player where to go next.

  1. You move

    Eight way stick plus one sword button. The software reads diagonals as their own inputs, and some rooms accept them.

  2. The Z80 checks the clock

    A 4 MHz Zilog Z80 runs 32 KB of program. For each scene it knows when the window opens, what input is right, and where each outcome lives on the disc.

  3. It sends a frame number

    A command goes to the Pioneer PR-7820 (early boards) or LD-V1000 (later boards): search to frame N, then play.

  4. The disc finds it

    The laser sled jumps across the disc to the frame. Short hop, quick. Long hop, a visible pause.

  5. The screen and LEDs

    Video goes straight to the CRT. In the US cabinet, score and lives sit on red LED digits above the screen. A tiny AY-3-8910 chip makes the beeps.

Right move, right time

Points, a confirming beep, the disc keeps rolling into the next part of the scene.

Wrong, early, or late

Search to the matching death clip, play it, search back. Early in a game you may get a random new room; late in the game you repeat the room you died in.

How long you actually get

Most moves allow more than 0.4 seconds. The nastiest ones, measured by the Dragon's Lair Project on the final ROMs, allow a tenth of a second. That is about three video frames.

Chapter 2

The six games

Same core idea, six very different machines. Three of them come from one man, Rick Dyer.

Dragon's Lair

1983 · THE ONE I SAW AT CHUCK E. CHEESE

Released
June 19, 1983
Made by
Advanced Microcomputer Systems (Rick Dyer) with Don Bluth's studio; sold by Cinematronics
Brain
Z80 at 4 MHz, 32 KB program ROM, AY-3-8910 sound chip
Disc player
Pioneer PR-7820 on ROM revisions A to D, Pioneer LD-V1000 from revision E. Philips 22VP932 in Europe.
Content
29 distinct rooms, 38 counting mirrored and repeat versions. More than 200 correct moves to finish.
Cost
50 cents a play, double the usual. Grossed about $32 million in its first eight months.
  • Mirrored rooms are real animation, not a hardware flip. Flipped copies are pressed on the disc, so the same room asks for the opposite directions.
  • The players were the weak spot. Constant seeking for hours a day burned them out. Later logic boards let operators switch from the PR-7820 to the LD-V1000, and ROM revision F stopped shuffling scene order so the laser would travel less.
  • Europe got more game at first. Atari's European version used rooms the US board skipped, like the drawbridge, and showed the score on its own screen between rooms. The US board filled those gaps with extra mirrored rooms until revision E added the drawbridge.
  • Shoestring cast. About a $1 million budget, so the animators voiced the characters. The only pro voice was narrator Michael Rye in the attract mode.
  • Where it came from. Dyer loved the text game Adventure and built a "Fantasy Machine" that told an illustrated story off a roll of paper. It never sold. After seeing The Secret of NIMH, he went to Bluth for real animation.
  • Operator secret. During attract mode, hold the stick Up Left and press Sword while dropping coins, release, then start one player. Unlimited lives.
  • First US finisher on record. Lance Mazmanian, 17, at Zody's Arcade in North Las Vegas in 1983. A rival finished one day later.

Space Ace

1984 · THE ONE WITH SKILL LEVELS

Released
Limited in December 1983, wide on April 29, 1984
Hardware
Same Cinematronics platform as Dragon's Lair, mostly sold as a conversion kit
Kit
New disc, new EPROMs, an extra "annunciator" board for the skill buttons, new art
Controls
Stick, one Fire/Energize button, three skill buttons
Skill levels
Cadet plays about half the scenes, Captain about two thirds, Space Ace all of them
  • The first real choice. At certain moments you can Energize and turn scrawny Dexter back into Ace. That sends the disc down a different branch. Skill level also changes which branches exist.
  • No annunciator board, no choice. Without that extra board the game just runs in Cadet.
  • Built with physical models. The team built Ace's starship and motorcycle as models, filmed them, then traced and painted the footage. A big tunnel model let a camera fly the dogfight.
  • The voices. Animator Jeff Etter is Ace, Will Finn is Dexter, Lorna Pomeroy is Kimberly, and Don Bluth himself is Borf.
  • 1991 remix. Leland put out a kit for Dragon's Lair II hardware that added diagonal moves and removed the easy levels. Ace only.
  • Sold roughly half as many machines as Dragon's Lair as the arcade boom faded.

Time Traveler

1991 · THE HOLOGRAM GAME

Made by
Sega, designed by Rick Dyer's Virtual Image Productions
Disc player
Sony LDP-1450
Display
A CRT aimed at a big concave mirror under a little stage
Controls
Stick, Action button, Time Reversal button
Story
Marshal Gram chases Vulcor through seven time periods to rescue Princess Kyi-La
  • It is not a hologram. A concave mirror focuses the monitor's picture into a real image that floats in the air over the stage. The cabinet is black inside so you cannot see the mirror doing it. Same physics as those mirror toys that make a coin float.
  • Filmed like a movie. Shot in San Diego with about 40 actors and a crew of around 100, mostly against green screen, then cut out so the actors stand on the little stage.
  • The rewind button, in 1991. The Time Reversal Cube rewinds to just before your death. You could buy more cubes between levels with coins.
  • Hidden dance. With a credit in, hold both buttons and pull the stick down. Rick Dyer dances around with his son on his back.
  • Reported about $18 million in sales. Sega later reused the cabinet for Holosseum, a fighting game with sprites instead of actors.
  • Home DVD and PC versions fake the effect with red and blue 3D glasses, since there is no mirror.

Dragon's Lair II

1991 · TIME WARP, THE TIME TRAVEL ONE

Animated
Started late 1983, stopped March 1984, finished in the late 80s
Released
1991 by Leland, the company that came out of Cinematronics
Disc player
Sony LDP-1450 or Pioneer 4200. Europe: Philips VP380.
Story
Mordroc steals Daphne through time; Dirk follows in a talking time machine
  • It shows you the answer. A quick yellow flash marks where to go or when to swing. The first game mostly made you guess.
  • Golden treasures. Hidden items need an extra move, often away from the flash. Miss any and the game loops you back to the first one you missed before it lets you finish.
  • Scrapped idea. Collecting every treasure was supposed to unlock a longer final battle. In the end everyone gets the long one.
  • The prototype had a completely different soundtrack.
  • Hal Smith voices Mordroc, Daphne's mother, the Time Machine, the Cheshire Cat and the card soldiers.
  • The home computer ports came out first and were built from a VHS tape of the animation, because no arcade disc existed yet.
  • The other time travel laserdisc game, Time Gal (Taito, 1985), plays today through Singe in Hypseus.

Us vs. Them

1984 · THE ONE ALMOST NOBODY PLAYED

Made by
Mylstar, the renamed Gottlieb. Programmer and video editor: Warren Davis, who made Q*bert.
Hardware
Built as a kit for M.A.C.H. 3 on the Mylstar Video Disc Graphics System
Display
Disc video underneath, computer drawn ships and shots on top
Survivors
About 13 known to collectors in the KLOV census
  • Two layers at once. Unlike Dragon's Lair, the computer draws sprites over the moving video. The overlay hardware came from M.A.C.H. 3, so the footage is the world and the sprites are the game.
  • A real film production. Aerial shoots over San Francisco, Chicago, Hollywood, forest, mountain and desert, custom sets for live action alien invasion scenes, comedy gags between waves, and an orchestral score.
  • Killed by timing. It tested well, then the laserdisc backlash hit. Players were breaking, operators were angry, orders were canceled, and lawsuits followed. Very few got built.
  • Warren Davis later built the video digitizing system behind Mortal Kombat and NBA Jam.

Thayer's Quest

1984 · THE ONE WITH A KEYBOARD

Made by
RDI Video Systems (Rick Dyer's renamed company)
Hardware
Conversion kit for Dragon's Lair and Space Ace cabinets; PR-7820 or LD-V1000
Controls
A membrane QWERTY keyboard with item keys. The first QWERTY keyboard on an arcade game.
Home twin
The RDI Halcyon: Z80B at 6 MHz, voice recognition, $2,500, never mass produced
  • An adventure, not a reflex test. You choose where to go and which magic item to use. Once you know a scene's answer it works every time.
  • It says your name. You type your name at the start and the speech synthesizer uses it. It also saved progress for the last ten players.
  • The original story. It is based on Shadoan, the big fantasy Dyer wrote before Dragon's Lair spun out of it. The arcade disc covers three of the five kingdoms.
  • What happened next. The rest arrived in the 90s as Kingdom: The Far Reaches and Kingdom 2: Shadoan, with the characters renamed.
  • The Halcyon disc is double sided and holds more material than the arcade disc.
  • Reportedly some arcades pulled it because kids typed swear words and the machine said them out loud.

Chapter 3

The lost version: more doors

Before release, Dragon's Lair was looser. Most rooms had several exits and several right answers. The sources do not say why it was cut back; the usual telling is that testers got lost. What is certain is that the cuts came late, after the animation was finished, because pieces of the old rooms are still hiding on the final disc.

Attract mode

A gargoyle throws a spear partway through. It was replaced by the Lizard King.

Still on the discTurn it up during the Lizard King shot and you hear the swish of the gargoyle's spear.

Vestibule

The ceiling does not fall right away. Dirk can leave left, leave right, or shove a log aside and drop through a trapdoor.

Still on the discThe trapdoor's hole is visible just before the floor crumbles. They removed the door but never painted floor over it.

Spiral Staircase

A red trip wire on the first step fires spears into Dirk. The goons also sounded different.

Still on the discThe wire was erased from the main scene but is still there in the death scene.

Wizard's Chamber

Slam the lid on the cauldron and run, or walk to three flasks: look at one, drink one, or pour one on your sword. Then fight, or leave by the door or a hole in the floor.

Still on the discThe floor hole and its ladder show up in the crumbling bones death.

Tentacles and Halberd

Mostly the same, plus cut moments like Dirk trying to pull the halberd off the rack, and a bloodier tentacle chop.

Tentacle room

No fire at all. Walk out the door ahead, or crawl through a hole behind the bench. Wait too long and a green tentacle drops from the ceiling.

Still on the discThat ceiling hole is where the fire comes from in the final game.

Mausoleum

No rolling skulls, no grabbing hand. Side doors lead to Crypt Creeps, and the door at the far end actually works.

Catwalk

Duck out early through a small opening on the left, or skip the catwalk and get chased by bats out a different door behind you.

Still on the discThe left opening appears in the crumbling bones death.

Yellow Brick Road

Choose left, right, or forward. Jump a wall of fire before the pool. Choose left or right again, then meet the spider without killing it.

Still on the discIn one death a knife reaches Dirk's back and vanishes a frame before it hits. The prototype let it land.

Mist Room

Only one snake. Nothing comes from the ceiling.

Still on the discThe ceiling hole for the second snake disappears as Dirk grabs the skull and leaves, because that ending was reused from the old version.

YMCA Room

An extra doorway on the left. Only the far stairs flatten, no worms in the pit, and an extra death where Dirk misses the chain.

Still on the discThe extra doorway is in the crumbling bones death.

Three Caves

No cage. Go left to the lava geyser bridge, right across stepping stones past a water monster, or forward through the jagged door.

Still on the discJust before the cage drops you can still see the side caves.

You can actually play these

The prototype footage was pressed onto a limited Dragon's Lair reproduction laserdisc in 2002 and added to the Dexter disc image. With the fan made Dragon's Lair Enhancement ROMs, a real cabinet will play the cut scenes. The Dragon's Lair Project prototype page has the clips and photos of the early cabinet, which had a black control panel with printed instructions and a single four digit score.

Chapter 4

Every move, every room

Do the inputs in order, each one at the moment the scene asks for it. Flashing objects usually point the way. Mirrored rooms swap left and right, so flip the switch when a room looks backwards.

L changes in the mirrored room + extra move on arcade Hard SWORD sword button

Room names and points follow the Dragon's Lair Project's arcade guide (points shown for the normal version, then mirrored). Move sequences come from a player guide for the HD release, which uses the arcade moves on Easy; I matched its room nicknames to the arcade names, and a couple of those matches are my best read. For frame exact timing, the Dragon's Lair Project Hard guide is the authority.

Practice the pattern

Pick a room and enter its moves with the pad, your arrow keys, or space for sword. This drills the order only; the real game also needs the timing.

Chapter 5

Dragon's Lair in 2027

The 1983 magic was two things: it looked like a movie, and a crowd could watch. The pain was invisible timing and rooms that were pass or die. Keep the first, fix the second, and bring back the prototype's doors.

Keep

  • Hand made frames. People crowded around for the art. AI video is great for quick animatics and test rooms, but lock a consistent, authored look for the final.
  • Watchability. That CRT on top of the cabinet was the original Twitch. Build a spectator view on purpose: TV mode, stream overlay, a crowd screen.
  • One input per moment. The fun is reading the scene and reacting. Don't bury it under menus.

Fix

  • Fair cues. Every move gets a tell in the art: a glint, a flash, a sound. Dragon's Lair II already did this in 1991.
  • Rewind instead of game over. Time Traveler's Time Reversal Cube is the template.
  • Generous windows by default. Offer the real 1983 timing, down to a tenth of a second, as an arcade mode.

Bring back

  • The prototype's choices. Two or three real exits per room, each its own clip. The castle becomes a map you learn over many runs.
  • Energize. Space Ace's risky transformation as a choice that opens harder, better paths.
  • The talking machine. Thayer's Quest said your name in 1984. Today's AI voices can narrate to you naturally, and the Halcyon's 1985 dream of voice commands finally works.
  • The hologram. A monitor under a concave mirror still floats a picture in midair. Cheap, and it still stops people.

How it runs now

No disc means no seek. Every clip loads ahead of time, so a branch is instant. The whole game becomes a scene graph: clips, timed input windows, and where each answer leads. This is the same table the 1983 Z80 held, just readable.

In a browser, an HTML video element plus requestVideoFrameCallback gives frame accurate windows on any phone. Singe 2 is an existing engine built for exactly this kind of game.

// one room, example values
{
  "room": "three_caves",
  "clip": "three_caves.webm",
  "moves": [
    { "at": 2.40, "window": 0.45,
      "cue": "left_cave_glows",
      "answers": {
        "L": "lava_bridge",
        "R": "water_stones",
        "U": "jagged_door" },
      "else": "death_cage" }
  ],
  "spectator": { "show_input": true }
}

A first build, in order

  1. Pick three rooms and storyboard them with two or three exits each.
  2. Make rough animatics with AI video to test the timing and cues.
  3. Write the scene graph and a tiny player that reads it.
  4. Add rewind, fair windows, and arcade mode as a switch.
  5. Add a spectator screen and put it in front of people.

A Decomp Lab target

The original program is only 32 KB of Z80 code, and somewhere in it is the scene table: frame numbers, window lengths, right answers, death clips. Load a ROM dump you own into Ghidra and you can read the game's brain directly. The fan Enhancement ROMs, which restored the prototype scenes, show it can be done.

Chapter 6

Play them today

Hypseus Singe

The maintained fork of the Daphne laserdisc emulator. Runs on Mac, Windows and Raspberry Pi, supports 4K video files, and runs Singe games like Time Gal. You need the ROM files plus the disc video as m2v and ogg files.

MAME

Covers several laserdisc games from disc images, including the overlay shooters like Us vs. Them.

Dexter

A replacement for the original player in a real cabinet. It plays a disc image and the game never knows the difference. Built by Matt Ownby and Warren Ondras over about eight years.

Official releases

Digital Leisure has kept Dragon's Lair, Space Ace, Dragon's Lair II, Time Traveler and Thayer's Quest in print for years on DVD, PC, Blu ray and consoles.

The movie

A live action Dragon's Lair film with Ryan Reynolds has been in development at Netflix for years, with James Bobin reported as director. I could not confirm a release date.

Dragon's Lair 3D

Bluth, Goldman and Dyer reunited for a fully 3D version in 2002 that let you walk the castle freely. A useful lesson in what to keep and what to change.
