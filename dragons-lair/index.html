The cut version

More doors, more choices, then they took them out

Before release, most rooms had several exits and more than one right answer. The cuts happened after the animation was finished, so pieces of the old rooms are still sitting on the final disc if you know where to look.

No skulls, no grabbing hand. Side doors lead to Crypt Creeps, and the far door actually opens.

All 30 rooms, in order of the inputs

Practice a room

Pick a room, then hit the pad, your arrow keys, or space for sword. This drills the order. The real thing also needs the timing.

Inside the cabinet

No graphics chip draws Dirk. It's all video.
  1. You move

    Eight way stick and one sword button. The program reads diagonals as their own inputs.

  2. The Z80 checks the window

    A 4 MHz Z80 runs 32 KB of program: for each scene, when the window opens, which input is right, and where each outcome sits on the disc.

  3. It sends a frame number

    The Pioneer player gets "search to frame N, play." Right answer, keep rolling. Wrong, jump to the death clip and back.

  4. Seeking kills players

    Hours of jumping wore the Pioneers out. Revision F stopped shuffling room order just to cut down the travel.

The other games

Same idea, five very different machines

1984 · Cinematronics · Don Bluth

Space Ace

  • Three skill buttons: Cadet plays about half the scenes, Captain about two thirds, Space Ace all of them.
  • The Energize button lets scrawny Dexter turn back into Ace, which sends the disc down a different branch.
  • Ace's ship and motorcycle were real models, filmed and then traced. A big tunnel model let the camera fly the dogfight.
  • Don Bluth himself voices Borf.

Same hardware as Dragon's Lair, sold mostly as a kit with an extra board for the skill buttons.

1991 · Sega · Rick Dyer · the hologram game

Time Traveler

  • Not really a hologram. A CRT shines into a big concave mirror under the stage, which floats a real image in midair. The black cabinet hides the mirror.
  • Shot in San Diego with about 40 actors, mostly on green screen.
  • The Time Reversal button rewinds to just before you die. A rewind button in 1991.
  • Hidden dance: with a credit in, hold both buttons and pull down. Rick Dyer dances with his son on his back.

Sony LDP-1450 player. Reported $18M in sales.

1991 · Leland · the time travel one

Dragon's Lair II: Time Warp

  • Animated starting in 1983, shelved in March 1984, finished years later, and released in 1991.
  • A quick yellow flash shows you where to go. The first game mostly made you guess.
  • Miss a hidden golden treasure and the game loops you back to it before it lets you finish.
  • The prototype had a completely different soundtrack.

Sony LDP-1450 or Pioneer 4200 player.

1984 · Mylstar · Warren Davis of Q*bert

Us vs. Them

  • Two layers: real aerial film on the disc, computer drawn ships and shots on top.
  • Live action alien invasion scenes on custom sets, with an orchestral score.
  • It tested well, then the laserdisc backlash hit. Orders were canceled and very few got built.

Warren Davis's GDC talk on making it (free)

1984 · RDI · Rick Dyer

Thayer's Quest

  • No joystick. A membrane QWERTY keyboard with item keys, the first on an arcade game.
  • You type your name and the speech chip says it during the game. It saved progress for the last ten players.
  • Built from Shadoan, the bigger story Dragon's Lair was spun out of.
  • Made for the Halcyon home system too: $2,500, voice recognition, never mass produced.

Making one in 2027

Keep the movie, fix the guessing, bring back the doors

Keep

  • Hand made looking frames. The art is why people crowded around.
  • A spectator view. The crowd watching was half the fun, so build a big screen mode on purpose.
  • One input per moment.

Fix

  • Fair cues for every move, like Dragon's Lair II's flash.
  • Rewind instead of game over, like Time Traveler's button.
  • Generous windows by default, real 1983 timing as an arcade mode.

Bring back

  • The prototype's two and three exit rooms, so the castle becomes a map you learn.
  • Space Ace's Energize as a risk choice.
  • A narrator who says your name, like Thayer's Quest did in 1984, with today's AI voices.
  • A concave mirror cabinet. Still cheap, still stops people.

The movie, and playing it now

The live action movie

Netflix and Ryan Reynolds announced a live action Dragon's Lair in 2020, and James Bobin was later reported as director. Nothing released yet that I could find.

Hypseus Singe

The maintained fork of the Daphne laserdisc emulator. Runs on Mac, Windows and Raspberry Pi. Needs the ROMs plus the disc video files.

Dexter

Swaps the worn out Pioneer in a real cabinet for a board that plays a disc image. The game never knows.

Official releases

Digital Leisure has kept Dragon's Lair, Space Ace, Dragon's Lair II, Time Traveler and Thayer's Quest in print on PC and consoles.
